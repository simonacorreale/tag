# Competitor — Esselunga a Casa

Direct chain: Esselunga runs its own online service. Extracted from the competitor table in [evidence.md](2026-10-10-desk-research-competitor-evidence.md) and the findings in [desk.md](../2026-10-10-desk-research-findings.md). The table row gives no source.

## Practice

| Dimension | What the desk research says |
|-----------|-----------------------------|
| Basket building | Wide selection from store catalogue; direct freshness control from central warehouse |
| Substitution control | Upfront choice: accept substitutions (selected by the picker) or do not substitute |
| Substitution notice | Notification via email/app once the receipt/order is finalised |
| Delivery windows | Precise 2-hour windows (e.g. 10:00–12:00) bookable in advance |
| At the door | Doorstep/floor delivery via proprietary refrigerated vans |

## Evidence from interviews

- **P1 Federica Franco**: main service for large stock-up orders. A mandatory tick box authorised substitutions, with no way to refuse. The app does not accept meal vouchers. New products arrive online months late. The wanted window must be booked 2–3 days ahead; once booked, it is always respected. Careful packaging; couriers carry the order to the door. See [P1 note](../../../../notes/2026-10-10-interview-note-federica-franco-p1.md).
- **P2 Luca Volpi**: the service he has used almost always in recent years, partly because of a UniCredit employee discount. Search returns too many unrelated results; sort by price per kilo appreciated. Missing or damaged items refunded immediately from a photo in the app. Found a next-day 18:00–20:00 window. Fresh products perceived as fresher than in store and than other services. See [P2 note](../../../../notes/2026-10-25-interview-note-luca-volpi-p2.md).

## Signals

- Contradiction with P1: the table says there is an upfront choice to accept substitutions or not; P1 says the tick box was mandatory. Both versions are kept. The table gives no source; P1 is recalling a past version of the app.
- Contradiction with P1: the table says freshness is controlled from a central warehouse; P1 says the order is picked from the nearest store. Both versions are kept. P2's view that fresh products are fresher because they are not on display fits the warehouse version.
- Shared observation, D5 and P2: Esselunga already offers locker pickup in some stores; P2 asks for a refrigerated locker for home delivery when nobody is in.
