# Interviews — Redesigning grocery delivery

Interview guide and record for the 3 interviews in the [research plan](plan.md). One interview has been held so far: P1, Federica, on 2026-10-10. It covers the whole guide in two recordings, except questions 7, 10, 12, 14, 17 and 18, which were not asked.

The interview was held in Italian. Everything in this file is in English: quotes and answers are translations. The original Italian wording is in the raw transcript, [interview-transcript.md](interview-transcript.md).

## Objective

Understand how people who do a large weekly grocery shop online experience the three moments in the [brief](brief.md) — building the basket, handling substitutions, receiving the order — and find out which one causes the most trouble.

## Format

- Semi-structured, 30 to 40 minutes, one participant at a time.
- 3 participants, one per day from 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-14. Profiles are in [plan.md](plan.md).
- Ask about the last real order, not about habits in general.
- Record with consent. Anonymise with participant codes.

## Interview guide

### Opening (3 min)

- Thank the participant, explain the purpose, ask for consent to record.
- "There are no right answers. I am interested in what actually happens when you order."

### Warm-up (5 min)

1. Who do you shop for?
2. How do you do your main grocery shop today? How much of it is online?
3. Which service do you use, and why that one?

### The last order (5 min)

4. Tell me about the last time you ordered groceries online. Start from the moment you decided to order.
5. What was the hardest part of that order?

### Building the basket (7 min)

6. How did you decide what to put in the basket?
7. How long did it take? Did you do it in one go?
8. How much of the basket is the same every week?
9. Was there anything you wanted and could not find?
10. Is there anything you never buy online? Why?

### Substitutions (8 min)

11. Has an item you ordered ever been unavailable? Tell me about the last time.
12. How and when did you find out?
13. What did you get instead? What did you do about it?
14. Did you tell the service in advance what to do if something was missing? How?
15. Are there items where you would accept an alternative, and items where you never would? What is the difference?

### Receiving the order (7 min)

16. How did you choose the delivery window? Did you get the one you wanted?
17. What happened on delivery day?
18. What did you check when the order arrived?
19. How were the fresh products? How does that compare with choosing them yourself?
20. Has anything ever gone wrong at delivery? What did you do?

### Closing (3 min)

21. If you could change one thing about ordering groceries online, what would it be?
22. Is there anything I did not ask that I should have?

### Hypotheses from desk research to probe

  Finding IDs refer to [desk.md](desk.md).

| ID | Hypothesis | Desk finding |
|----|------------|--------------|
| H1 | People accept substitutions for most items but have a few they will not compromise on. | D11 |
| H2 | Setting substitution preferences item by item is too much work, so people leave the default. | D7, D9 |
| H3 | A substitute is acceptable when it is the same brand or something bought before. | D12 |
| H4 | Finding out at the door is worse than finding out before delivery. | D18, D19 |

## Cross-interview synthesis

Provisional: based on one interview (P1). To be revised after P2 and P3. "Strength" says how firmly P1's own account supports the theme, not how common it is.

| Theme | Moment | Participants | Evidence | Strength |
|-------|--------|--------------|----------|----------|
| Substitution consent is all or nothing: one mandatory tick box, no way to refuse or to set exceptions | Substitutions | P1 | Q11, Q5 | Strong, recollection not verified against the current app |
| Most items can be substituted; a few cannot. The line is price and taste, not category | Substitutions | P1 | Q13, Q15 | Strong |
| For the items that matter, removal with the price taken off is preferred to any substitute | Substitutions | P1 | Q15, Q21 | Strong |
| The wanted fix is upstream: accurate stock, so an unavailable item cannot be ordered | Substitutions, Building the basket | P1 | Q21 | Medium, stated once |
| Paying first and waiting for a refund is disliked | Substitutions | P1 | Q21 | Medium, stated once |
| The app is uncomfortable to browse; quantities for loose produce are confusing | Building the basket | P1 | Q21 | Medium, stated once |
| A core of the basket repeats every order | Building the basket | P1 | Q8 | Medium |
| Fresh produce and meat are mostly bought in store; online only pre-packed | Building the basket | P1 | Q19 | Medium |
| Promotions with free delivery shape the big order | Building the basket | P1 | Q3, Q4 | Medium |
| The wanted delivery window must be booked days ahead | Receiving the order | P1 | Q16 | Medium |
| Delivery itself works: on time, well packed, helpful couriers | Receiving the order | P1 | Q20 | Strong |
| Trust in fresh quality depends on who picks the order | Receiving the order | P1 | Q19 | Weak, partly second-hand |

**Moment with the most friction:** handling substitutions, for P1. She named it unprompted as the hardest part (Q5) and again as the first thing she would change (Q21). Building the basket comes second, because of the app layout. Receiving the order was positive apart from booking the window.

**Insights to carry into the moment selection on 2026-10-16:**

1. The problem is control, not substitution itself. P1 is relaxed about most swaps and upset only where she had no say.
2. A short list of protected items would cover her need: a few products, defined by price and taste, that must never be substituted.
3. For protected items the acceptable outcome is "leave it out and do not charge me", not a better substitute.
4. Prevention ranks above repair for her: stock shown in the app should match the store that picks the order.
5. One gap remains for all three interviews: how and when the shopper learns about a substitution. Ask question 12 explicitly to P2 and P3.

These rest on one participant who is recalling a past habit. They agree with the desk research on H1 and differ on H2 (see [desk.md](desk.md), D7 and D11).
