# Interviews — Redesigning grocery delivery

Interview guide and record for the 3 interviews in the [research plan](plan.md). One interview has been held so far: P1, Federica, on 2026-10-10. It covers the whole guide in two recordings, except questions 7, 10, 12, 14, 17 and 18, which were not asked.

The interview was held in Italian. Everything in this file is in English: quotes and answers are translations. The original Italian wording is in the raw transcript, [interview-transcript.md](interview-transcript.md).

## Objective

Understand how people who do a large weekly grocery shop online experience the three moments in the [brief](brief.md) — building the basket, handling substitutions, receiving the order — and find out which one causes the most trouble.

## Format

- Semi-structured, 30 to 40 minutes, one participant at a time.
- 3 participants, one per day from 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-14. Profiles are in [plan.md](plan.md).
- Ask about the last real order, not about habits in general.
- Record with consent. Anonymise with participant codes.

## Interview guide

### Opening (3 min)

- Thank the participant, explain the purpose, ask for consent to record.
- "There are no right answers. I am interested in what actually happens when you order."

### Warm-up (5 min)

1. Who do you shop for?
2. How do you do your main grocery shop today? How much of it is online?
3. Which service do you use, and why that one?

### The last order (5 min)

4. Tell me about the last time you ordered groceries online. Start from the moment you decided to order.
5. What was the hardest part of that order?

### Building the basket (7 min)

6. How did you decide what to put in the basket?
7. How long did it take? Did you do it in one go?
8. How much of the basket is the same every week?
9. Was there anything you wanted and could not find?
10. Is there anything you never buy online? Why?

### Substitutions (8 min)

11. Has an item you ordered ever been unavailable? Tell me about the last time.
12. How and when did you find out?
13. What did you get instead? What did you do about it?
14. Did you tell the service in advance what to do if something was missing? How?
15. Are there items where you would accept an alternative, and items where you never would? What is the difference?

### Receiving the order (7 min)

16. How did you choose the delivery window? Did you get the one you wanted?
17. What happened on delivery day?
18. What did you check when the order arrived?
19. How were the fresh products? How does that compare with choosing them yourself?
20. Has anything ever gone wrong at delivery? What did you do?

### Closing (3 min)

21. If you could change one thing about ordering groceries online, what would it be?
22. Is there anything I did not ask that I should have?

### Hypotheses from desk research to probe

  Finding IDs refer to [desk.md](desk.md).

| ID | Hypothesis | Desk finding |
|----|------------|--------------|
| H1 | People accept substitutions for most items but have a few they will not compromise on. | D11 |
| H2 | Setting substitution preferences item by item is too much work, so people leave the default. | D7, D9 |
| H3 | A substitute is acceptable when it is the same brand or something bought before. | D12 |
| H4 | Finding out at the door is worse than finding out before delivery. | D18, D19 |

## Interview record

### P1 — Federica — 2026-10-10

**Profile:** Federica, 30. Shops for herself and her partner. Used Esselunga online 2 to 3 times a month when she lived in Milan without a car. She now has a car and orders online less often. Video call.

**Coverage:** the whole guide, in two recordings (questions 1 to 9, then 11 to 22). Questions 7, 10, 12, 14, 17 and 18 were not asked; where another answer touches them, this is noted below. Most answers describe her past habit in Milan, not a recent order, and she does not remember which item was last substituted.

**Last order in brief:** a bulk order of heavy items — milk, drinks, detergents — made from the app because she was in Milan without a car. She checked for brand promotions that give free delivery before ordering.

**Building the basket**
- Observations:
  - She orders online for two reasons: tiredness after the working week, and heavy items she cannot carry on foot.
  - Orders were large, "around 350" (she does not say the unit; probably euros).
  - Before a big order she looks for brand promotions that give free delivery.
  - A core of the basket is always the same: Esselunga's Smart own-brand line (cured meats, grated cheese, flour). She still buys these in store.
  - The assortment is wide enough. What is missing are newly launched products, which arrive months late.
  - The Esselunga app does not accept meal vouchers. That is why she used Glovo a couple of times.
  - She buys little fresh produce or meat online, because she prefers to choose it herself in store. When she did, she chose pre-packed items, not ones sold by weight.
  - She finds the app uncomfortable to shop in: products are hard to see on the page, and setting the quantity for loose fruit and vegetables was confusing.
- Quotes:
  - "It is really uncomfortable to shop from the apps, the products are hard to see on the pages."
  - "When you have to buy fresh fruit and vegetables and you had to enter the quantity, it was really awkward, you could not understand anything."
  - "After a week of work I did not want to go to the supermarket at the weekend as well, which is even worse."
  - "We definitely always bought Esselunga's Smart brand."
  - "If you see a product that has just come out, you do not find it straight away. You have to wait a few more months."

**Substitutions**
- Observations:
  - She raised substitutions herself, unprompted, as the hardest part of ordering (question 5).
  - When the brand she chose was unavailable, she felt she had no choice but to accept Esselunga's basic own-brand product instead.
  - She mentions the price difference, but it is not clear who paid it or in which direction.
  - She describes it as a stress that came back with every order.
  - To place an order she had to tick a box authorising the service to substitute unavailable products. She describes it as mandatory, with no way to refuse.
  - She does not remember which item was last substituted. The service left free samples in the order, which she read as an apology.
  - Her reaction was calm. For most items, the same product from another brand was fine. For missing items she contacted Esselunga and was refunded.
  - A few items are not negotiable: Coca-Cola (any Coca-Cola variant is fine, Pepsi is not) and Nutella. Her rule: items she pays a lot for, or where she cares about the taste.
  - For those items she would rather not receive the product and have the price taken off than receive a substitute.
  - This qualifies her answer to question 5: the stress is tied to having no say, and to the few items she cares about, not to substitutions in general.
- Quotes:
  - "You had to tick the box saying they were authorised to substitute your products if they were not available."
  - "When they replaced the product with the same thing from a different brand, I did not mind, because I am not too fussy about these things."
  - "Coca-Cola and Pepsi are two completely separate worlds for me."
  - "If I pay a lot for it, I do not accept that you substitute it. I would rather you leave it out and take the price off what I paid."
  - "The hardest part in general, or anyway the part that gets on your nerves, was that if that brand was not there, you had to accept the basic Esselunga one as a substitute, paying the price difference of course."
  - "That is the stress I had every time I did a shop."

**Receiving the order**
- Observations:
  - Getting the delivery window she wanted was hard. She ordered two or three days ahead, or more, to secure it; otherwise she took whatever was left.
  - Once booked, the window was always respected.
  - No problems at delivery. Packaging was careful, and couriers were kind and carried the order up to her door.
  - The pre-packed fresh products she ordered were always good, for both quality and expiry date. She thinks she was lucky: she knows people who were not, and says it depends on who picks the order.
- Quotes:
  - "Very often it is hard to get the window you want. Either I ordered early or I never got the window I wanted, so I had to make do with the ones available."
  - "When they give you a window, they always respect it."
  - "I think I was also lucky, because I know people who did not have a good experience. And it depends a lot on who does your shopping."

**Most painful moment, in the participant's words:** substitutions. "That is the stress I had every time I did a shop."

**One thing they would change:** first, limit substitutions by keeping the app's stock aligned with the store the order is picked from, and removing items from the app as they run out. Second, the layout of the app, which she finds uncomfortable.

**Hypotheses:**
- H1 — supported. She accepts another brand for most items and names two she will not compromise on, Coca-Cola and Nutella.
- H2 — not supported as worded. She describes a single mandatory tick box, with no item-by-item preferences to set. Her problem is the absence of control, not the effort of using it.
- H3 — mixed. For most items a different brand is fine. For the few she cares about, only the exact product is acceptable.
- H4 — no evidence. She did not say how or when she found out about a substitution.

**Interviewer notes:**
- She regularly buys Esselunga's own-brand Smart line by choice, yet dislikes receiving an own-brand product as a substitute. The problem may be the lack of choice, not the own brand itself. This is an interpretation.
- Her proposed fix is upstream of substitution: accurate stock in the app, so the unavailable item cannot be ordered at all. She also dislikes paying first and waiting for a refund.
- Friction is concentrated before delivery: the app layout, the substitution consent, booking a window. The delivery itself was positive on every point she mentioned.
- Still unclear: what "paying the price difference" means, and how and when she found out about a substitution.
- Her description of the mandatory tick box is her recollection of the Esselunga app at the time. It is not verified against the current app.
- Topics outside the three moments: promotions as a reason to order, and meal vouchers as a reason to use another service.
- She suggested two questions for the next interviews: how accurately the order was packed, and how one supermarket's service compares with another's.

#### P1 — Answers mapped to the guide

**Opening.** The interviewer explained the purpose and said there were no right or wrong answers. Consent to record is not audible in the recording.

**Q1. Who do you shop for?**
"My family used to be just me, now it is me and Michele, so yes a family, but a small one." Asked how many people: "Two."

**Q2. How do you do your main grocery shop today? How much of it is online?**
"Less than before, because now I have a car. But when I lived in Milan I did it online at least twice a month, two or three times a month at least."

**Q3. Which service do you use, and why that one?**
"I used Glovo a couple of times because there were offers and I could pay with meal vouchers, but usually Esselunga online, even if [that was] when there were offers, like when you bought a certain amount of a brand and got free delivery or some offer, because you cannot use meal vouchers on the Esselunga app."

**Q4. Tell me about the last time you ordered groceries online.**
"The last time I shopped online it was because we wanted to stock up on milk, drinks, lots of heavy things anyway, detergents too, and being in Milan without a car we did not feel like going on foot."
Asked what happened in the app: "I opened the app, I placed the order, I checked whether there was some offer, because we bought things like milk, drinks, detergents, heavy things in bulk, so large quantities. I usually always checked whether there was an offer like, to give you an example, Mutti: buy 20 euros of Mutti products and you get free delivery. So I usually checked those things when I did a big shop, also the last time I did it."

**Q5. What was the hardest part of that order?**
"The hardest part in general, or anyway the part that gets on your nerves, when I placed those orders, was that if that brand was not there, you had to accept the basic Esselunga one as a substitute, paying the price difference of course. That is the stress I had every time I did a shop."

**Q6. How did you decide what to put in the basket?**
"Usually when I shopped online it was either out of laziness, simply because after a week of work I did not want to go to the supermarket at the weekend as well, which is even worse. Or because when I did it online I knew I had to buy a lot of heavy things, and being on foot I did not feel like making several trips or carrying so much. I did some big shops online, even around 350."
Note: she answered why she orders online, not how she chooses the items.

**Q7. How long did it take? Did you do it in one go?**
Not asked.

**Q8. How much of the basket is the same every week?**
"If we are talking about Esselunga [unclear], we definitely always bought Esselunga's Smart brand. The shop never lacked cured meats, grated cheese, those Smart-brand things. There was always something Smart, flour, anything. Those were never missing, and even now that we go in person, so not from the app, those things are never missing from the trolley."

**Q9. Was there anything you wanted and could not find?**
"Do more affordable prices count as something to find?" Asked about products: "Usually, at least at Esselunga, you find quite a lot. What you do not find, generally, are the new products. If you see a product that has just come out, you do not find it straight away, you have to wait a few more months. To give you a silly example, some new ice creams had come out, I do not remember the brand, maybe the Kinder Bueno ones. And Esselunga got them at the end of summer, when nobody was eating them any more. But otherwise no, I generally find everything."

The first recording stops here. The second recording starts at question 11.

**Q10. Is there anything you never buy online? Why?**
Not asked. See the answer to Q19: she buys little fresh produce and meat online.

**Q11. Has an item you ordered ever been unavailable? Tell me about the last time.**
"Yes, it happened, because as I mentioned it used to happen at Esselunga: when you shopped online you had to tick [source transcribed as 'sleggare', read as 'flaggare'] the box saying they were authorised to substitute your products if they were not available. So yes, it happened, I do not remember with which item because too much time has passed. But to, let's say, make up for it, to thank you, they left you a few samples of brands or of new things coming out in store, like snack bars, little samples of jam, of Nutella."

**Q12. How and when did you find out?**
Not asked. The interviewer treated it as already answered, but no answer in either recording says how or when she found out.

**Q13. What did you get instead? What did you do about it?**
"I did not get angry, in the sense that I generally understood. Only in the case of missing items did I contact Esselunga and get a refund. But generally, when they replaced the product with the same thing from a different brand, I did not mind, because I am not too fussy about these things."

**Q14. Did you tell the service in advance what to do if something was missing? How?**
Not asked. See the answer to Q11: she describes a mandatory tick box authorising substitutions.

**Q15. Are there items where you would accept an alternative, and items where you never would? What is the difference?**
"There are some things. The example that comes to mind straight away is Coca-Cola. Coca-Cola, whether it is regular or Zero, is fine, but if you give me Pepsi I start to like it less. Or Nutella too: that time you buy it and it costs you six euros, and you give me an off-brand one, that is not fine. So generally we can say the items you pay a bit more for, or the ones where you care more about the taste, because Coca-Cola and Pepsi are two completely separate worlds for me. Those are the first two examples that come to mind, but generally the expensive items: if I pay a lot for it, I do not accept that you substitute it. I would rather you leave it out and take the price off what I paid."

**Q16. How did you choose the delivery window? Did you get the one you wanted?**
"Very often, to get my delivery window, the one I wanted, I ordered early, meaning I started ordering much sooner. Say I wanted it delivered on Friday afternoon, because I worked the morning shift or was free anyway. I did the online shop two or three days before, even a bit more, because very often it is hard to get the window you want, I do not know, because of staff, because of their own problems. Usually either I ordered early or I never got the window I wanted, so I had to make do with the ones available."

**Q17. What happened on delivery day?**
Not asked. See the answer to Q20.

**Q18. What did you check when the order arrived?**
Not asked.

**Q19. How were the fresh products? How does that compare with choosing them yourself?**
"[unclear] I bought little fresh food like fruit and vegetables, because I generally like to go and choose it in the shop. A packaged product is one thing, fruit and vegetables or meat are another: those I prefer to look at myself. But the times I did buy them, I usually did not take the ones that had to be weighed, I took the pre-packed ones, like the pre-weighed pack of carrots, courgettes, salad and so on. I was always happy, both with the expiry date and with the quality of the product. But I think I was also lucky, because I know people who did not have a good experience. And it depends a lot on who does your shopping, of course."

**Q20. Has anything ever gone wrong at delivery? What did you do?**
"No, no problems, to tell you the truth, because usually when they give you a window they always respect it, they never go outside the time slot. And the packaging of the products was very well done: every bottle of tomato passata was put in a sort of paper bag lined with bubble wrap. So luckily I had no problems, really none. The couriers were very kind too, they brought it all the way up, they helped me, really no problems."

**Q21. If you could change one thing about ordering groceries online, what would it be?**
"I would definitely put a limit on the whole 'I will substitute the product if it is not there' thing. I would propose an alternative: fix the app so that it more or less keeps the stock up to date in line with the store's sales, because the shopping is taken from the nearest store anyway, not from Esselunga in general. Because as I said before, if I pay 6 euros for an item, for example Nutella which costs a lot, I do not want you to substitute it, or to have to wait for you to give me the money back after I have paid because it is not there. Simply, as items run out, if things really do run out, because supermarkets have good stocks, you take them off the app [source transcribed as 'il leverage dalle app', read as 'li levi dalle app']. And then the layout in general, definitely, because it is really uncomfortable to shop from the apps, the products are hard to see on the pages. I would make the view a bit cleaner, a bit lighter. Also when you have to buy fresh fruit and vegetables and you had to enter the quantity, it was really awkward, you could not understand anything."

**Q22. Is there anything I did not ask that I should have?**
"You did not ask me whether the shopping was delivered accurately, I mean the packing of the shopping, but I do not know if that is too much. You did not ask me to compare with another supermarket, because I stopped at Esselunga, but another person might have ordered from several supermarkets, because many offer it now. So I would tell you to ask about the difference between one supermarket and another, what can be improved by comparing them."

**Moment with the most friction:** handling substitutions, for P1. She named it unprompted as the hardest part (Q5) and again as the first thing she would change (Q21). Building the basket comes second, because of the app layout. Receiving the order was positive apart from booking the window.

**Insights to carry into the moment selection on 2026-10-16:**

1. The problem is control, not substitution itself. P1 is relaxed about most swaps and upset only where she had no say.
2. A short list of protected items would cover her need: a few products, defined by price and taste, that must never be substituted.
3. For protected items the acceptable outcome is "leave it out and do not charge me", not a better substitute.
4. Prevention ranks above repair for her: stock shown in the app should match the store that picks the order.
5. One gap remains for all three interviews: how and when the shopper learns about a substitution. Ask question 12 explicitly to P2 and P3.

These rest on one participant who is recalling a past habit. They agree with the desk research on H1 and differ on H2 (see [desk.md](desk.md), D7 and D11).
