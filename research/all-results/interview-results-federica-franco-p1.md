# Interview results — P1 Federica Franco

What the interview with P1 Federica Franco (2026-10-10, video call) shows, moment by moment. Built from two files only: the note `interview/interview-note-federica-franco-p1.md` and the transcript `interview/interview-transcript-p1.md`.

Every result names the guide question it comes from (Q1 to Q22) and the recording (R1 or R2) where the words can be checked. Quotes are English translations of the Italian transcript.

## Participant

- P1 Federica Franco, 30. Household of two, with her partner.
- Ordered from Esselunga online 2 to 3 times a month while living in Milan without a car. She now has a car and orders less. (Q2, R1)
- Used Glovo a couple of times, for offers and because it accepts meal vouchers. (Q3, R1)

Limit: most answers describe a past habit, not a recent order.

## Main result

Substitutions are the moment that hurts. She named them without being asked, as the hardest part of ordering (Q5, R1), and again as the first thing she would change (Q21, R2). The cause is having no say, not the substitution itself: she accepts most swaps and objects only for a few products she cares about.

## Answers to the research questions

## Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Result |
|----|------------|--------|
| H1 | People accept substitutions for most items but have a few they will not compromise on. | Supported (S4, S5). |
| H2 | Setting preferences item by item is too much work, so people leave the default. | Not supported as worded. She had one mandatory box and no item-level preferences (S1). |
| H3 | A substitute is acceptable when it is the same brand or bought before. | Mixed. Brand matters only for the few protected products (S4, S5). |
| H4 | Finding out at the door is worse than finding out before delivery. | No evidence. The question was not asked. |
