# Desk research results

What the desk research shows, moment by moment, and how it compares with the first two interviews. Built from `raw/raw-docs/desk/2026-10-10-desk-research-findings.md` (findings D1 to D22), `raw/raw-docs/desk/competitors/2026-10-10-desk-research-competitor-evidence.md` (competitor table), the four competitor files in `raw-docs/desk/competitors/` and the interview results of P1 and P2.

Every result names the findings it comes from (D1, D2, ...). Sources (S1, S2, ...) are in `raw/raw-docs/desk/competitors/2026-10-10-desk-research-sources.md`.

Results for each competitor are in separate files: [Esselunga a Casa](2026-10-10-desk-research-results-esselunga-a-casa.md), [Coop / EasyCoop](2026-10-10-desk-research-results-coop-easycoop.md), [Everli](2026-10-10-desk-research-results-everli.md), [Tesco / Ocado](2026-10-10-desk-research-results-tesco-ocado.md).

## Main result

Substitutions are the best documented pain point of online grocery, but almost all the evidence is from the UK and the US (D6 to D19). Shoppers accept most substitutes and protect a few items (D11); the interfaces to say which are tedious (D7, D9). Evidence on building the basket, delivery windows and freshness is thin, and Italian consumer data on the three moments is missing.

The two interviews do not confirm substitutions as the main pain point: P1 says it is, P2 barely notices them and struggles with search and delivery windows instead.

### Handling substitutions

| ID | Result | Evidence |
|----|--------|----------|
| S1 | Substitutions are hard: 76% of usability-test participants had some difficulty, among the hardest scenarios tested. | D6 |
| S2 | Setting preferences item by item is tedious. Suggested fix: one global preference, or asking only about items at risk. | D7, D9 |
| S3 | Asking only about at-risk items needs near real-time stock data. | D8 |

## Comparison with the interviews

| Desk says | P1 Federica Franco | P2 Luca Volpi |
|-----------|--------------------|---------------|
| Substitutions are the main pain point (S1) | Agrees: the hardest part and the first thing she would change | Disagrees: often does not notice them; search is the hardest part |
| Most items substitutable, a few protected (S4) | Agrees: Coca-Cola and Nutella protected | No protected item named; Q15 not asked |
| Brand drives acceptance (S6) | Only for the protected items | Not brand-loyal; chooses by promotion |
| Item-by-item preferences are tedious (S2) | Not tested: she had one mandatory box | Not asked |
| Notice before delivery matters (R1) | Not asked | Not asked; he often does not notice |
| Basket building: no evidence (B1) | App layout uncomfortable | Search and choice overload, over 20 minutes |
| Delivery windows: no evidence (R5) | Books days ahead to get one | Cannot commit days ahead; wants a refrigerated locker |
| Freshness: no evidence (R5) | Good, buys only pre-packed | Fresher than store shelves |

The interviews fill the desk gaps on basket building, windows and freshness. Both participants use Esselunga, so the comparison says nothing yet about the other services.

## Contradictions

| Desk says | Other source says |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Esselunga offers an upfront choice to accept substitutions or not (evidence.md) | P1: the tick box was mandatory, no way to refuse. Her memory of a past app. |
| Esselunga controls freshness from a central warehouse (evidence.md) | P1: the order is picked from the nearest store. P2's view that products are fresher because not on display fits the warehouse version. |
| Ocado missing/error rates below 1% (evidence.md) | D14: 19% of Ocado shoppers had a substitution in their last shop. May measure different things. |

All versions are kept. The competitor table gives no source for any row.

## What the desk research does not tell

- How Italian services handle unavailable items today: options, notice, refund.
- Whether substitution works differently with a marketplace shopper.
- Delivery fees, minimum spend and window availability in Italy.
- Anything on basket building, choosing windows, missed deliveries or freshness.
- Italian consumer data on any of the three moments.

## Limits

Most substitution evidence is from the UK and the US. Many findings are marked as not yet checked against the original source, and the Esselunga findings may be dated (D3, D4). The competitor table has no sources. D19 is marked "UA" in desk.md, probably a typo for US. No conclusion can be drawn for the Italian market without first-hand checks.
