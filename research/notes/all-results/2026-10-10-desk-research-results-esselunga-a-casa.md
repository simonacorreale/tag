# Desk research results — Esselunga a Casa

Direct chain with its own online service. The only service both interview participants use, so the only one where desk claims can be checked against shoppers. Built from `raw/raw-docs/desk/competitors/2026-10-10-desk-research-esselunga-a-casa.md`, the competitor table in `raw/raw-docs/desk/competitors/2026-10-10-desk-research-competitor-evidence.md`, findings D3, D4, D5, D21 in `raw/raw-docs/desk/2026-10-10-desk-research-findings.md`, and the results of P1 and P2.

## Main result

On paper, Esselunga covers the three moments well: an upfront substitution choice, 2-hour windows, refrigerated vans. The interviews confirm the delivery and the fresh quality, but contradict the substitution control (P1) and show friction the desk research does not cover: search (P2) and booking a window in advance (P1, P2).

## Results by moment

### Building the basket

| ID | Result | Evidence |
|----|--------|----------|
| E-B1 | Wide selection from the store catalogue. Fill the basket on site or app, then choose a window. A Fìdaty card is required. | evidence.md; D3 (may be dated) |
| E-B2 | Search returns many unrelated results and is the hardest part of ordering for P2. Not in the desk research. | P2 B1, B2 |
| E-B3 | Sort by price per kilo exists and is appreciated by P2. | P2 B6 |
| E-B4 | The app is uncomfortable to browse; it does not accept meal vouchers; new products arrive months late. | P1 B5, B6, B8 |

### Handling substitutions

| ID | Result | Evidence |
|----|--------|----------|
| E-S1 | Upfront choice: accept substitutions chosen by the picker, or do not substitute. | evidence.md, no source |
| E-S2 | P1 remembers a mandatory tick box with no way to refuse. Contradicts E-S1. | P1 S1 |
| E-S3 | Notice by email or app once the order is finalised. | evidence.md, no source |
| E-S4 | Missing or damaged items are refunded immediately from a photo in the app. | P2 S3 |

### Receiving the order

| ID | Result | Evidence |
|----|--------|----------|
| E-R1 | 2-hour windows bookable in advance; delivery hours reported as 7:00 to 23:00 Monday to Saturday, 8:30 to 20:00 Sunday. | evidence.md; D4 (may be dated) |
| E-R2 | The wanted window must be booked days ahead (P1); P2 cannot commit days ahead to be home. | P1 R1; P2 R5 |
| E-R3 | Delivery to the floor in refrigerated vans. Both participants confirm delivery to the door, on time, with no problems. | evidence.md; P1 R2, R3; P2 R2, R3 |
| E-R4 | Store pickup and lockers exist in some stores; P2 asks for a refrigerated locker for home delivery. | D5; P2 R6 |
| E-R5 | Freshness controlled from a central warehouse. P2 finds fresh products fresher than store shelves; P1 finds pre-packed fresh always good. | evidence.md; P1 R4; P2 R4 |

## Contradictions

| Desk says | Interviews say |
|-----------|----------------|
| Upfront choice to accept substitutions or not | P1: a mandatory tick box, no way to refuse. Her memory of a past app. |
| Freshness controlled from a central warehouse | P1: the order is picked from the nearest store. P2's reason for freshness ("not on display") fits the warehouse version. |

Both versions are kept. The table row gives no source.

## What is not known

- The current substitution options in the app. Needs a first-hand walkthrough.
- Delivery fees and minimum spend.
- Whether orders are picked in store or in a warehouse, and where.

## Limits

The table row has no source; D3 and D4 may be dated. Interview evidence comes from two participants.
