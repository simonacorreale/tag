# Interview notes — P1 Federica Franco

## Profilo partecipante
- **Nome / ID**: P1 Federica Franco
- **Nucleo familiare**: 2 persone (lei e il partner Michele).
- **Contesto**: Precedentemente residente a Milano senza auto (alta dipendenza dalla spesa online); attualmente con auto, frequenza online ridotta ma ancora presente.

---

## Abitudini di acquisto e servizi utilizzati
- **Frequenza**: In passato a Milano 2–3 volte al mese; spese di grandi dimensioni (anche fino a ~350€).
- **Piattaforme**:
  - **Esselunga online**: servizio principale per carrelli grandi/stock, sfruttando promozioni (es. soglie per spedizione gratuita). Nota negativa: impossibilità di usare buoni pasto in app.
  - **Glovo**: utilizzato saltuariamente per offerte e possibilità di pagare con buoni pasto.
- **Driver di utilizzo**:
  - Acquisto di prodotti pesanti/ingombranti in stock (latte, bibite, detersivi) per evitare il trasporto a piedi.
  - Risparmio di tempo ed evitamento del supermercato affollato nel weekend dopo la settimana lavorativa.

---

## Articoli ricorrenti e reperibilità
- **Sempre presenti nel carrello**: Prodotti a marchio del distributore (linea Esselunga Smart: salumi, formaggio grattugiato, farina).
- **Reperibilità**: Generalmente buona; il disservizio principale riguarda le novità commerciali (es. nuovi gelati) che arrivano online con mesi di ritardo rispetto al lancio.

---

## Gestione out-of-stock e sostituzioni (Pain Point Principale)
- **Frustrazione sulle sostituzioni forzate**: Obbligo di accettare sostituzioni generiche o dover deselezionare opzioni; stress legato alle differenze di prezzo e ai rimborsi.
- **Categorie non sostituibili**:
  - Prodotti premium / brand specifici legati al gusto personale (es. *Coca-Cola* vs *Pepsi*, *Nutella* vs sottomarca/private label). In questi casi preferisce lo storno/mancata consegna rispetto a un'alternativa non gradita.
- **Categorie sostituibili**: Beni generici o di base, dove il brand non incide sull'esperienza d'uso.

---

## Scelta dello slot e logistica di consegna
- **Fasce orarie**: Difficoltà a trovare lo slot desiderato (es. venerdì pomeriggio). Necessità di pianificare e inoltrare l'ordine con 2–3 giorni di anticipo per garantirsi la fascia.
- **Esperienza di consegna**: Molto positiva. Puntualità rispettata, imballaggio accurato (bottiglie in vetro protette con pluriball) e corrieri disponibili a portare i carichi al piano.

---

## Prodotti freschi
- **Comportamento**: Scetticismo verso i freschi sfusi da pesare (preferisce sceglierli di persona al banco).
- **Compromesso online**: Acquisto limitato a prodotti freschi già confezionati e pre-pesati (es. carote in busta, insalate pronte). Nessun problema riscontrato su qualità e scadenze.

---

## Opportunità di miglioramento emerse (Feature & UX)
1. **Sincronizzazione scorte in tempo reale**: Aggiornare la disponibilità in app con lo stock effettivo del punto vendita di riferimento per evitare rotture di stock a monte.
2. **Politica di rimborso trasparente**: Storno immediato del prodotto mancante senza dover attendere rimborsi successivi.
3. **Riprogettazione UI carrello e catalogo**: Migliorare la leggibilità dell'interfaccia (layout più pulito) e semplificare l'inserimento quantità per i prodotti freschi.

## Interview record

### P1 Federica Franco — 2026-10-10

**Profile:** P1 Federica Franco, 30. Shops for herself and her partner. Used Esselunga online 2 to 3 times a month when she lived in Milan without a car. She now has a car and orders online less often. Video call.

**Coverage:** the whole guide, in two recordings (questions 1 to 9, then 11 to 22). Questions 7, 10, 12, 14, 17 and 18 were not asked; where another answer touches them, this is noted below. Most answers describe her past habit in Milan, not a recent order, and she does not remember which item was last substituted.

**Last order in brief:** a bulk order of heavy items — milk, drinks, detergents — made from the app because she was in Milan without a car. She checked for brand promotions that give free delivery before ordering.

**Building the basket**
- Observations:
  - She orders online for two reasons: tiredness after the working week, and heavy items she cannot carry on foot.
  - Orders were large, "around 350" (she does not say the unit; probably euros).
  - Before a big order she looks for brand promotions that give free delivery.
  - A core of the basket is always the same: Esselunga's Smart own-brand line (cured meats, grated cheese, flour). She still buys these in store.
  - The assortment is wide enough. What is missing are newly launched products, which arrive months late.
  - The Esselunga app does not accept meal vouchers. That is why she used Glovo a couple of times.
  - She buys little fresh produce or meat online, because she prefers to choose it herself in store. When she did, she chose pre-packed items, not ones sold by weight.
  - She finds the app uncomfortable to shop in: products are hard to see on the page, and setting the quantity for loose fruit and vegetables was confusing.
- Quotes:
  - "It is really uncomfortable to shop from the apps, the products are hard to see on the pages."
  - "When you have to buy fresh fruit and vegetables and you had to enter the quantity, it was really awkward, you could not understand anything."
  - "After a week of work I did not want to go to the supermarket at the weekend as well, which is even worse."
  - "We definitely always bought Esselunga's Smart brand."
  - "If you see a product that has just come out, you do not find it straight away. You have to wait a few more months."

**Substitutions**
- Observations:
  - She raised substitutions herself, unprompted, as the hardest part of ordering (question 5).
  - When the brand she chose was unavailable, she felt she had no choice but to accept Esselunga's basic own-brand product instead.
  - She mentions the price difference, but it is not clear who paid it or in which direction.
  - She describes it as a stress that came back with every order.
  - To place an order she had to tick a box authorising the service to substitute unavailable products. She describes it as mandatory, with no way to refuse.
  - She does not remember which item was last substituted. The service left free samples in the order, which she read as an apology.
  - Her reaction was calm. For most items, the same product from another brand was fine. For missing items she contacted Esselunga and was refunded.
  - A few items are not negotiable: Coca-Cola (any Coca-Cola variant is fine, Pepsi is not) and Nutella. Her rule: items she pays a lot for, or where she cares about the taste.
  - For those items she would rather not receive the product and have the price taken off than receive a substitute.
  - This qualifies her answer to question 5: the stress is tied to having no say, and to the few items she cares about, not to substitutions in general.
- Quotes:
  - "You had to tick the box saying they were authorised to substitute your products if they were not available."
  - "When they replaced the product with the same thing from a different brand, I did not mind, because I am not too fussy about these things."
  - "Coca-Cola and Pepsi are two completely separate worlds for me."
  - "If I pay a lot for it, I do not accept that you substitute it. I would rather you leave it out and take the price off what I paid."
  - "The hardest part in general, or anyway the part that gets on your nerves, was that if that brand was not there, you had to accept the basic Esselunga one as a substitute, paying the price difference of course."
  - "That is the stress I had every time I did a shop."

**Receiving the order**
- Observations:
  - Getting the delivery window she wanted was hard. She ordered two or three days ahead, or more, to secure it; otherwise she took whatever was left.
  - Once booked, the window was always respected.
  - No problems at delivery. Packaging was careful, and couriers were kind and carried the order up to her door.
  - The pre-packed fresh products she ordered were always good, for both quality and expiry date. She thinks she was lucky: she knows people who were not, and says it depends on who picks the order.
- Quotes:
  - "Very often it is hard to get the window you want. Either I ordered early or I never got the window I wanted, so I had to make do with the ones available."
  - "When they give you a window, they always respect it."
  - "I think I was also lucky, because I know people who did not have a good experience. And it depends a lot on who does your shopping."

**Most painful moment, in the participant's words:** substitutions. "That is the stress I had every time I did a shop."

**One thing they would change:** first, limit substitutions by keeping the app's stock aligned with the store the order is picked from, and removing items from the app as they run out. Second, the layout of the app, which she finds uncomfortable.

**Hypotheses:**
- H1 — supported. She accepts another brand for most items and names two she will not compromise on, Coca-Cola and Nutella.
- H2 — not supported as worded. She describes a single mandatory tick box, with no item-by-item preferences to set. Her problem is the absence of control, not the effort of using it.
- H3 — mixed. For most items a different brand is fine. For the few she cares about, only the exact product is acceptable.
- H4 — no evidence. She did not say how or when she found out about a substitution.

**Interviewer notes:**
- She regularly buys Esselunga's own-brand Smart line by choice, yet dislikes receiving an own-brand product as a substitute. The problem may be the lack of choice, not the own brand itself. This is an interpretation.
- Her proposed fix is upstream of substitution: accurate stock in the app, so the unavailable item cannot be ordered at all. She also dislikes paying first and waiting for a refund.
- Friction is concentrated before delivery: the app layout, the substitution consent, booking a window. The delivery itself was positive on every point she mentioned.
- Still unclear: what "paying the price difference" means, and how and when she found out about a substitution.
- Her description of the mandatory tick box is her recollection of the Esselunga app at the time. It is not verified against the current app.
- Topics outside the three moments: promotions as a reason to order, and meal vouchers as a reason to use another service.
- She suggested two questions for the next interviews: how accurately the order was packed, and how one supermarket's service compares with another's.

**Q1. Who do you shop for?**
"My family used to be just me, now it is me and Michele, so yes a family, but a small one." Asked how many people: "Two."

**Q2. How do you do your main grocery shop today? How much of it is online?**
"Less than before, because now I have a car. But when I lived in Milan I did it online at least twice a month, two or three times a month at least."

**Q3. Which service do you use, and why that one?**
"I used Glovo a couple of times because there were offers and I could pay with meal vouchers, but usually Esselunga online, even if [that was] when there were offers, like when you bought a certain amount of a brand and got free delivery or some offer, because you cannot use meal vouchers on the Esselunga app."

**Q4. Tell me about the last time you ordered groceries online.**
"The last time I shopped online it was because we wanted to stock up on milk, drinks, lots of heavy things anyway, detergents too, and being in Milan without a car we did not feel like going on foot."
Asked what happened in the app: "I opened the app, I placed the order, I checked whether there was some offer, because we bought things like milk, drinks, detergents, heavy things in bulk, so large quantities. I usually always checked whether there was an offer like, to give you an example, Mutti: buy 20 euros of Mutti products and you get free delivery. So I usually checked those things when I did a big shop, also the last time I did it."

**Q5. What was the hardest part of that order?**
"The hardest part in general, or anyway the part that gets on your nerves, when I placed those orders, was that if that brand was not there, you had to accept the basic Esselunga one as a substitute, paying the price difference of course. That is the stress I had every time I did a shop."

**Q6. How did you decide what to put in the basket?**
"Usually when I shopped online it was either out of laziness, simply because after a week of work I did not want to go to the supermarket at the weekend as well, which is even worse. Or because when I did it online I knew I had to buy a lot of heavy things, and being on foot I did not feel like making several trips or carrying so much. I did some big shops online, even around 350."

"I would definitely put a limit on the whole 'I will substitute the product if it is not there' thing. Simply, as items run out, if things really do run out, because supermarkets have good stocks, you take them off the app [source transcribed as 'il leverage dalle app', read as 'li levi dalle app']. And then the layout in general, definitely, because it is really uncomfortable to shop from the apps, the products are hard to see on the pages. I would make the view a bit cleaner, a bit lighter. Also when you have to buy fresh fruit and vegetables and you had to enter the quantity, it was really awkward, you could not understand anything."
